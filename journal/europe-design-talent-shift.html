Why Europe's best design talent isn't where you'd assume

Aoibh Journal7 min read

Ask most people where to find design talent in Europe and you'll hear the same two or three cities every time. The actual growth data tells a different story: between 2020 and 2025, design employment in London grew by just 3% — while Belfast's grew by 15%, twice the pace of Northern Ireland's overall job growth. The center of gravity is shifting, and it isn't shifting toward where most people assume.

Berlin: still the design capital, now an AI capital too

Berlin's design industry generated €13.8 billion in turnover in 2024, and the city marked 20 years as a UNESCO City of Design this year. What's changed is what sits alongside that heritage now: Berlin's startup ecosystem ranks among the strongest in the world, with a dedicated Berlin AI Hub backed directly by the city government. Design here isn't separate from the AI/tech scene — increasingly, it's built inside it.

Belfast: the growth story nobody's talking about

This is the real surprise in the data. Northern Ireland's design sector grew against the UK-wide trend — the number of design firms is up 10% since 2021, and design employment is up 15% since 2020, twice the growth rate of Northern Ireland's economy overall. That's backed by real infrastructure, not just momentum: a £13 million investment in Future Screens NI (a Ulster University and Queen's University Belfast collaboration) and a separate £72 million investment in Studio Ulster, a large-scale virtual production complex. Belfast isn't an "emerging" market anymore so much as one that's already arrived and is still under-discussed.

London and Paris: still the anchors, not where the new momentum is

London and Paris remain enormous by scale — the UK design economy alone contributes £136.7 billion in gross value added, and France's creative industries generated €102.7 billion in 2024. Neither city is going anywhere. But the growth curve has clearly moved elsewhere: London's own design employment growth (3%, 2020–2025) badly lags other UK regions, some of which grew 20%+ in the same window. Scale and momentum have stopped being the same thing.

Copenhagen: the quality benchmark

Denmark's creative industries contribute around 6% of GDP and employ over 100,000 people, built on a design tradition — the Royal Danish Academy, Arne Jacobsen, Hans Wegner — that still shapes how the rest of the world thinks about considered, functional design. Copenhagen also leads on sustainability specifically, with Copenhagen Fashion Week widely regarded as the industry's sustainability benchmark. It's a smaller market than London or Paris by pure size, but an outsized influence on design standards generally.

The old model — one or two obvious capitals, everyone else a satellite — doesn't match where the actual growth, investment, and talent development is happening anymore.

What this means if you're hiring design talent

Defaulting to the "big name" city isn't wrong, exactly — it's just no longer the whole picture. The strongest match for a specific brief might sit in Belfast rather than London, or Copenhagen rather than Paris, and increasingly the data backs that up rather than just intuition. A network built around where the real talent and momentum actually are, rather than a single flagship city, reflects how the map has already changed — not a bet on how it might change later.
