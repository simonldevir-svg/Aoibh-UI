Why the Gulf's creative economy boom is going AI-native

Aoibh Journal7 min read

The UAE and Saudi Arabia aren't just spending more on design — they're building entire economic strategies around it, backed by real government money and real targets, not vague ambition. What's less talked about is how that work is actually getting made: AI-assisted, remote-first production is already the mainstream direction in both markets, not a future trend a vendor is trying to talk you into.

The UAE's creative economy, by the numbers

The UAE's creative economy grew 12% annually between 2019 and 2023 — outpacing the country's overall GDP growth over the same period. Dubai's own target is to double the creative industries' contribution to the city's GDP to 5% by 2026, generating 140,000 jobs along the way. The design industry specifically generated AED 18 billion in revenue in 2023, contributing 2.5% of national GDP, with architectural design alone accounting for 40% of that figure.

The digital and tech side of the creative industry is growing even faster: AED 45 billion in revenue in 2023, at a 15% compound annual growth rate. This isn't a market catching up to demand — it's one actively outrunning it.

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 bet on design

Saudi Arabia's approach is more explicit still. Vision 2030 names the creative sector as a direct priority for economic diversification, targeting 3% of GDP by 2030 — roughly $20 billion in revenue and 100,000 jobs — up from under 0.5% when the program began. Design sits alongside film, publishing, fashion, and gaming as one of the sectors the Ministry of Culture is actively funding, regulating, and building infrastructure for, not simply hoping will grow on its own.

That's a meaningfully different starting point than most Western markets, where creative services demand tends to follow existing business growth. Here, the demand is being deliberately created as national policy — which means the businesses operating in these markets right now are building their creative supply chains from a comparatively blank slate.

The part most agencies miss: it's already going AI-native

By 2023, AI adoption across the UAE's creative industry had already reached 60%, with 40% of firms using AI specifically for content creation. Separately, hybrid and remote hiring models became mainstream across the UAE from 2023 onward, with businesses reporting real cost savings — commonly cited around 50% — versus hiring full-time, in-house designers.

Two of the fastest-growing creative economies in the world are already defaulting to AI-assisted, remote-first production. That's not a pitch — it's just where the market already is.

Put together, that's a market where AI-assisted drafting, human art direction, and a distributed rather than office-bound team aren't a workaround — they're becoming the standard way the work actually gets done. A studio built natively around that model isn't offering something unfamiliar to a business in Dubai, Riyadh, or Abu Dhabi right now; it's offering the same shape of production the market is already moving toward, just built for it from the start rather than retrofitted onto an older agency structure.

What this actually means if you're evaluating options

If you're a business in the UAE or Saudi Arabia weighing a traditional local agency against a remote, AI-assisted studio, the honest framing isn't "which is more innovative" — it's that the remote, AI-assisted model is already where a majority of the region's own creative industry has moved. The real questions worth asking are the same ones that matter anywhere: who reviews the AI-assisted output before it ships, how designers are actually matched to a brief rather than just available, and whether you can see real progress on your project rather than waiting for a status email.
